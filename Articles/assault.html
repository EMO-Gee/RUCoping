Know Your Rights

  • The Right to Safety: You have the right to a learning environment free from violence and intimidation.
  • The Right to Medical Care: You can seek medical attention at any facility without being forced to file a police report.
  • The Right to Confidentiality: Many campus counselours are "confidential resources", meaning they wont report your identity without your permission.
  • The Right to Accommodations: If an assault affects your studies, you may have the right to "No Contact Orders" or academic extensions.
  • The Right to Report: You have the right to report to the police, to the University, to both, or to neither. The choice is yours.

Physical Assault

Physical assault occurs when an individual intentionally applies force to another person without their consetn, or attempts/threatens to do so in a way that causes the victim to fear immediate violence. This ranges from a push or a slap (Common Assault) to more severe attacks causing permanent injury (Grievous Bodily Harm). In a campus setting, this often involves physical altercations during social events or targeted bullying.

What To Do First

  1. Get to Safety: Move to a well-lit, populated area or a locked room.
  2. Check for Injuries: If you are bleeding or feel dizzy, go to the nearest ER.
  3. Preserve Evidence: Take photos of your injuries and the scene if it is safe to do so. Do not clean or throw away torn or bloodied clothing.
  4. Seek Medical Care: Even if injuries look minor, internal bruising or concussions need a professional check-up.

Sexual Assault

Sexual assault is any form of unwanted sexual contact or activity, that happens without explicit consent. This includes non-consensual touching, forced sexual acts, or taking advantage of someone who is unable to give consent (e.g. due to intoxication). Consent must be enthusiastic, concious, and can be withdrawn at any time.

What To Do First

  1. Prioritise a "Safe Space": Find a friend you trust or a campus advocate.
  2. Avoid Bathing/Changing: If you might want a forensic exam (rape kit), try not to shower, eat, drink, or brush your teeth until you are seen by a professional.
  3. Forensic Examination: Go to a hospital with a SANE (Sexual Assualt Nurse Examiner). You can usually do this anonymously.
  4. Emergency Contraception: Seek medical advice within 72 hours regarding STI prevention and pregnancy options.

Psychological Assault

Psychological assault involves a systematic pattern of non-physical behaviours used to manipulate, intimidate, or control another person. This includes "gaslighting" (making a victim doubt their own perceptions), constant verbally abusive language, social isolation, and threats of self-harm or exposure to control the victim's actions. In a student environment, this often manifests as a partner or a "friend" group monitoring your communications, spreading rumours to destroy your reputation, or using your academic insecurities against you.

What To Do First

  1. Recognise the Abuse: Acknowledge that the behaviour is not your fault and that you deserve respect.
  2. Set Boundaries: If possible, clearly communicate to the abuser that their behaviour is unacceptable and must stop.
  3. Seek Support: Talk to trusted friends, family members, or a campus counsellor about what you're experiencing.
  4. Document Incidents: Keep a record of abusive interactions, including dates, times, and descriptions of what happened. This can be helpful if you decide to report the abuse later.
