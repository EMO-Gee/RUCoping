Information and support for those affected by HIV

First Thing First!

First, what is HIV?

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)
HIV is a virus that attacks cells that help the body fight infection, making a person more vulnerable to other infections and diseases. It is spread by contact with certain bodily fluids of a person with HIV. If left untreated, it can lead to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome).

And sadly the human body can't get rid of the HIV and no effective HIV cure exists.
BUT there are very effective drug treatments that enable most people

How can HIV be contracted

HIV is contracted through infected bodily fluids such as semen, vaginal fluids, blood, breast milk and anal mucous. The most common ways HIV is transmitted are through unprotected sex and through sharing needles.
You cannot get HIV through:

  • Hugging, kissing, or shaking hands with someone who has HIV.
  • Sharing food or drinks.
  • Using the same toilet or shower.
  • Being bitten by a mosquito.
  • Having unprotected sex with someone who is living with HIV but is "Undetectable".

Prevention:

  • Use condoms consistently and correctly.
  • Get tested regularly and know your partner's status.
  • Consider pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) if at high risk.

PrEP

What is PrEP?
PrEP(pre-exposure prophylaxis) is the use of anti-HIV medication that keep HIV-negative people from getting HIV

How offten do I need to take PrEP

You need to take it once a day at more or less the same time. You can take it within a few hours of your normal time, as long you only take one pill a day.

Does PrEP have side effects?

Some People get mild side effects when they start PrEP.
The most common side effects include:

  • Nausea
  • Headache
  • Tiredness
  • Diarrhoea
  • Problems sleeping
  • Change in appetite
In most people, these side effects go away after a few weeks.

Think you might have contracted HIV?

If you think you might have contracted HIV, it's important to get tested ASAP!
Early detection can lead to better health outcomes and reduce the risk of transmission to others. You can get tested here at Rhodes University Health Care Clinc or at any local clinc.
Why must I rush and get tested? if I might already have it?
Early detection allows for timely treatment, which can help manage the virus and maintain a healthy immune system. It also reduces the risk of transmitting HIV to others. As early treatment can reduce your viral load, so low that it will be Undetectable in your system. Being Undetectable means you can not transmit the virus to others through unprotected sex.

Exposed to HIV within 72 Hours?

If you were exposed to the HIV virus within 72 hours. RUN TO YOU NEAREST CLINIC!
A nurse would would administ a medication called PEP

What is PEP?
PEP or Post Exposure prophylaxis is an emergency treatment that is given to a person exposed to HIV to prevent HIV

Who should take PEP?

Anyone who may have been exposed to HIV through contact with blood, bodily fluids, during sex or through their work.
It's only recommended for people who are HIV negative or don't know their status.
